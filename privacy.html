The short version: Seize apps are built to work without your data. We don't run accounts, we don't use advertising SDKs, and we don't sell or share anything. Some apps use RevenueCat for purchase analytics only (see Atino, Cycle Timers, GamingHub, Tempo, Roomy, Readably, Yaybo and, from version 0.1.2, Overbit). What you put into an app stays on your device and in your own iCloud unless the app's own section below says otherwise (see GamingHub, Atino, Overbit, Tempo, Cycle Timers, BeatRide and Readably: its documents stay on the iPhone and are not synced to iCloud).

Garum is the exception: it has accounts, a server in the EU and user content, so it has its own privacy policy and terms of use.

Atino is another exception: your CV, redacted on your iPhone, is sent to third-party ranking services through a Seize relay when you allow it, and, once you have consented, its subscription is reported to RevenueCat for purchase analytics, so it has its own privacy policy.

Overbit is another exception: to send you alerts it registers a random install identifier and your push-notification token with a Seize server and, from version 0.1.2, its purchases are reported to RevenueCat for purchase analytics, so read its section below.

GamingHub is another exception: to run a room it sends your display name, avatar and what you type in a game to a Seize server (Supabase), and its purchases are reported to RevenueCat for purchase analytics, so read its section below.

Tempo is another exception: it uses your location on your iPhone, Apple's maps service receives the places you search for and the coordinates of your first home and work zones, and its purchases are reported to RevenueCat for purchase analytics, so it has its own privacy policy.

Cycle Timers is another exception: its purchases are reported to RevenueCat for purchase bookkeeping and, up to version 0.0.22, what you wrote in Message the developer (Cycle Pro) was sent to a server of ours, so it has its own privacy policy.

Roomy is another exception: from version 0.0.3 its purchases are reported to RevenueCat for purchase analytics, so it has its own privacy policy (English).

Readably is another exception: it downloads its on-device voices once from Hugging Face when you ask for them, Readably Pro's “Read from a link” fetches the one web page you name, and it uses RevenueCat for purchase analytics. No account, no server of ours, and the text you read never leaves your iPhone. Read its section below.

What we collect

Nothing, by default. Our apps do not require an account, do not include advertising or tracking SDKs (the only third-party analytics is RevenueCat's purchase analytics in Atino, Cycle Timers, GamingHub, Tempo, Roomy, Readably, Yaybo and, from version 0.1.2, Overbit, see their sections), and do not transmit the content you create to us. We cannot see your timers, work sessions, expenses, receipts, routines, or any other content you create in a Seize app. Yaybo is the exception: a note a grown-up chooses to write and an anonymous Apple ad token reach a Seize server, and, only if a grown-up turns on Read-aloud, the device listens to a child, without recording, while a button is held (see Yaybo).

Purchases are processed entirely by Apple. Where an app sells a one-time "Pro" unlock, the app checks that purchase with Apple's StoreKit on your device and remembers only whether it is active; we never see who bought it (Atino, Cycle Timers, GamingHub, Tempo, Roomy, Readably and, from version 0.1.2, Overbit also report purchases to RevenueCat for analytics; see their sections). We receive aggregated, anonymized sales and crash statistics from Apple's App Store Connect (for example, the number of downloads by country). These statistics contain no personal identifiers.

Purchases. In the apps published by Sendoa Sola (Drip, Anchor, Kover, Tandem, Meso, Grain, Logicle and Heed), when you buy something (a tip, "Pro", an unlock or a subscription), start a trial or redeem a code, Apple tells us, and also about its renewals and refunds: which product, when, in which App Store country, the price and currency, and some transaction numbers and a pseudonym that Apple generates for each account and app. It does not include your name, your email or your Apple ID. We keep it on a server of our own, only to keep our accounts and to handle refunds or questions about a purchase. We do not share it with anyone or use it for advertising or analytics. We keep it for six years, the time the law requires for bookkeeping (basis: complying with our legal bookkeeping obligations, and our legitimate interest in handling questions and refunds). Nothing changes for the apps published by Izotz Cristobal Mota: what their sections say about RevenueCat stays the same.

Where your data lives

  • On your device. App content is stored locally, in the app's private container (and, for apps with widgets, a shared App Group container that only the app and its own widget can read).
  • In your iCloud. Apps that sync do so through your personal iCloud account under Apple's terms, unless you turn it off in Settings or the app's own section below says otherwise — we never have access to it. Cycle Timers, Tempo, Readably, Atino, Roomy, GamingHub and Overbit do not sync through iCloud.
  • On our server (only where an app's section says so). Yaybo keeps the notes a grown-up chooses to send and the answer to which ad brought the install, for 12 months, on a Seize server in Frankfurt, Germany; the text of a note is also forwarded to a private Telegram chat that only the developer reads and is deleted there automatically after one month (see Yaybo).

Heed

Heed has no account system, no analytics, no ads, no third-party code that receives your data and no server of ours: nothing you do in Heed is sent to us. It keeps two kinds of data, in two different places.

What you write. Your settings and what you write in Heed — your values and your own sentence, your apps, the day's intentions, your alternatives, the line for each day, the closings of the day and the weekly reviews — are stored on your device and, with iCloud sync, which is on by default, in the private database of your personal iCloud account (CloudKit), under Apple's terms; we never have access to it. Turning it off in Settings keeps them on the device only.

The record of your openings. Each time one of the apps you chose opens, Heed records which app and when, the window it showed (if any), the option you picked, how it ended and how many seconds you took, and it works out from that which windows help you. This record and what Heed learns from it stay only on your device, in the app's private container and a private App Group container shared only with Heed's own widgets and Shortcuts action; they never go to iCloud and are deleted after 90 days. Heed can export them as a JSON file, but only when you ask for it, and the file goes only where you share it.

Shortcuts and your other apps. Heed works through automations you switch on in Apple's Shortcuts app ("When X is opened"): Shortcuts starts Heed and passes it the name of the app, and that is all. Heed cannot see what you do in other apps, what is on their screen or which apps you have installed. To take you back after "Continue", Heed may open that app's link (its URL scheme, or its web address as a universal link), with nothing about you attached; the first time, iOS asks whether Heed may open it.

Widgets, Live Activity and notifications. If you add them, the widgets show today's intention on the Home Screen or the Lock Screen, and during a focus block the Live Activity shows the minutes left. On the Lock Screen the intention stays hidden until the device is unlocked; the minutes left can be read by anyone who sees your screen. When a focus block ends, Heed shows a local notification if you have allowed it; it is scheduled on the device, with no server involved.

Apple Health. Off by default. If you switch it on in Settings, Heed writes mindful minutes when you finish a practice or a guided breath, and the day's mood (State of Mind) when you close the day. Heed never reads anything from Health and never uploads Health data anywhere; the mood goes only to Health, never to iCloud through Heed.

Apple processes them; we never see your payment details. What Apple tells us about each purchase is under Purchases.

Heed is a wellbeing tool, not a medical device or a treatment, and it is not directed at children. Questions about Heed and your data: hello@seizeapps.com.

BeatRide

BeatRide builds indoor cycling classes from music. Your rider profile (level, age or maximum heart rate if you give them, and the answers to the health check), the marks you put on songs and the analysis of each song (tempo, energy and its shape) stay on your device, in the app's private container. Your class history syncs through your personal iCloud account when iCloud sync is on (the default), under Apple's terms; we never have access to it. BeatRide has no analytics, no advertising and no third-party code that receives your data.

Your name and username. When you create your profile, your display name and the username you choose are stored in BeatRide's public iCloud database (Apple CloudKit), with your username reserved so nobody else can take it. Today nothing in the app shows them to other people. Deleting your account in Settings deletes both.

Apple Music. With your permission BeatRide reads your playlists and their songs to build classes, plays them through Apple Music, and creates a playlist in your library only when you ask it to. When BeatRide picks the music, it asks Apple Music for songs by their ISRC code. Playback follows Apple's terms; we never see your Apple Music account.

Measuring songs. To know a song's tempo, energy and shape, BeatRide downloads its 30-second Apple Music previews, measures them on the device and throws them away; it sends the song's title, artist and length to LRCLIB (a public database of song timings), from which it uses only the timings. Nothing about you goes with those requests. BeatRide also ships maps of song structure from public research datasets.

Microphone. Only Learn by listening uses it, while you run it: BeatRide plays a playlist out loud and keeps measurements of what it hears (levels and frequency bands, ten times a second) to learn each song's shape. Sound is never recorded or uploaded.

Apple Health. Off by default. If you switch it on, each class you finish is written to Health as an indoor cycling workout (start and end). BeatRide never reads anything from Health and never uploads Health data anywhere.

Notifications

Apps that remind you of things use local notifications, scheduled and delivered on your device. We do not operate push-notification servers for these features. Overbit is the exception: it registers with a Seize server so that the server can push its alerts.

Your rights

Because your data lives on your device, you exercise your rights directly: deleting content in an app deletes it; deleting the app deletes its container; turning iCloud sync off and deleting the app removes it from iCloud as well (or manage it under Settings › Apple Account › iCloud). Under the GDPR you also have rights of access, rectification, erasure, and portability — since we hold no personal data about you, there is typically nothing for us to produce, but you can always reach us with questions or requests at hello@seizeapps.com. The exception is Overbit, which keeps a record on our server under a random identifier: delete it in the app (Settings › Your data › Delete my data) or ask us there. Yaybo keeps a note you send and its ad-attribution answer on our server for 12 months: ask us to delete them sooner; everything else in Yaybo you delete in its Parent Zone (a child's "Delete…", or Settings › Delete all data). For the record RevenueCat holds from version 0.1.2, write to us. Purchase notices (see Purchases) carry no name or email: to find the ones for a purchase we need its order ID, which is on the receipt Apple sends you.

Children

Our apps are general-audience utilities and do not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, including children. Readably keeps the text you read on your iPhone. The exception is Yaybo, which is made for children under 13 and used with a grown-up: it is built to collect no personal information from them, and its own privacy policy explains how.

Changes

If a future app or feature changes any of the above (for example, an app that adds an online service), we will update this policy and the app's App Store privacy label before that feature ships.
